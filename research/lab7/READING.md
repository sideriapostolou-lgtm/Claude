# Lab 7: reading the TRAIN results (2026-10-11)

`RESULTS.md` is generated by `results.py` from the result files; this note is the plain reading of the same numbers
for the owner, plus what lab 7 did not test.

## The idea

The owner asked: instead of buying near-certain outcomes at 97c (lab 4: no edge), make each desk a specialist in its
market that buys when a contract is cheap, say at 50c, sells a little higher, say at 55c, cuts losses quickly and
keeps strict risk limits. Lab 7 tested exactly that, on six weeks of real Polymarket trades (July 1 to August 15,
the TRAIN months), with the rules written down and committed before any result was seen (`PLAN.md`, each
`<W>/PREREG.md`).

## What the idea needs to work

Polymarket US charges `0.0695 x p x (1 - p)` per contract every time you buy or sell (taker fee). Near the middle
of the price range that is about 1.7c a side.

| trade (per $1 contract) | buy fee | sell fee | you keep if it works | you lose if you cut the loss the same distance away | must be right |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| buy 50c, take profit at 53c | 1.74c | 1.73c | **-0.47c** (a "win" that loses) | -6.47c | never |
| buy 50c, take profit at 55c | 1.74c | 1.72c | +1.54c | -8.46c | 85 of 100 |
| buy 50c, take profit at 60c | 1.74c | 1.67c | +6.59c | -13.41c | 67 of 100 |

On top of the fee, you buy at the asking price and sell at the bid. On these markets the gap between the two was
about 1c (sports during games) to 1.5c (non-sports), so the real hurdle is higher still. **So a specialist has to be
right far more than half the time**: about 85 times in 100 for the 50c-to-55c trade. A high win rate alone proves
nothing, because take profit and stop settings can produce one by luck. That is why every rule also had to beat
random buys with the same exits.

## What we found, market by market

| desk | what it tried | versions | made money after the fee | best version: right / needed | best version, per $1 traded | before any fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 sports before the game | buy when Polymarket is 2-5c below the sharpest bookmaker's (Pinnacle's) fair price, sell 2-5c higher or at the fair, always before the start | 54 | 0 | 6 % / 54 % | -8.9c | -1.7c |
| W2 crypto up/down windows (5 min, 15 min, 1 h) | buy when 3-8c below a fair price worked out from Bitcoin's live price, sell 3-8c higher or at the fair | 54 | 0 | 54 % / 66 % | -7.1c | -0.75c |
| W3 sports during the game | buy after a 5-10c jump in 2-5 minutes (or bet against the jump), take profit 3-10c, cut the loss at 5-10c | 288 | 0 | 43 % / 66 % | -6.9c | -0.8c |
| W4 politics, weather, culture, economics, finance | the same over hours (a 5-10c move over 1-4 hours) | 192 | 0 | 44 % / 62 % | -8.7c on real fills (-4.0c with impossible fills) | -2.5c on real fills |

None of the 588 versions passed, so the next month (VAL) and the final three weeks (TEST) were never run for any
desk. W1's best version "won" only 6 % of its trades because a 2c take profit loses money once both fees are paid.
W2's before-fee figure is the skeptic's (`W2/SKEPTIC.md`). W4's real-fill figures keep only buys made inside its
price band. The plan fills a whole $20 order at one trade's price, whatever that trade's size, and outside the band
this produced fills that could not have happened, such as 20,000 contracts at 0.1c off a 40-contract trade
(`W4/train_diag.md`).

Why, in plain words:

- **W1 (sports before the game).** When Polymarket was cheaper than the bookmaker, the gap usually did not close
  before the game started. Polymarket's traders often knew something (a lineup, an injury). Every version lost even
  before fees.
- **W2 (crypto windows).** The "fair price" from Bitcoin's live price turned out to be a worse forecast than the
  market's own price, so the cheap-looking side was usually the model's mistake. These short windows are not listed
  on Polymarket US anyway.
- **W3 (sports in play).** A team whose price jumps does keep drifting up a little, about 1.4c to 3.4c. But most of
  that happens within seconds, and the price you can buy at already includes it. Even with perfect execution (buy
  at the exact signal price, sell at exactly the target or the stop), all 288 versions still lose after the fee.
  Being ten times faster changed nothing.
- **W4 (non-sports).** Same story with smaller numbers. After a move, the price drifts on by about 0.6c to 2c, mostly
  within 10 minutes. With perfect execution the best version still loses 2.6c per $1.

**The common thread:** the prices are already about right. A buy plus a sell costs about 3.4c per contract in fees
near 50c, plus the 1-1.5c gap between the buy and sell prices. The moves any desk could catch were smaller than that.

## Did good risk management help?

It made the losses smaller. It never made money.

- **Cutting losses (stops)** never turned a losing rule into a winner. In crypto the stop made the loss bigger in
  every one of the 36 versions that had one (the W2 skeptic corrected the specialist's summary on this). Elsewhere
  it moved the loss by less than 1c per $1 either way. Tight stops made the total loss bigger, because the desk sold out
  and bought back in more often.
- **A $10-a-day loss limit** shrank the totals a lot, but only by trading less. It still lost money: W1 -$239 over
  133 trades, W2 -$1,216 over 1,485, W3 -$861 over 594, W4 -$2,413 over 1,412. Without the limit, the best versions
  of W1, W2 and W3 lost money on every day they traded.
- Risk management decides how much you lose when you are wrong. Only an edge decides whether you win.

## How sure we are

- Each desk's result was redone from scratch by a separate skeptic, with its own program on the raw trade records.
  W1: 158 of 160 trades identical; the other 2 came from a team-name mix-up inherited from lab 6 (Los Angeles FC
  vs LA Galaxy), and fixing it changes nothing. W2: all 50,934 trades identical. W3: 32,053 trade comparisons with
  0 differences. W4: 2,245 trades with 0 differences. No bug was hiding a winner.
- Every version tried is counted: 610 in lab 7 (588 selectable rules plus 22 hold-to-the-end references), 0 passes,
  3,661 tries across labs 2-7.
- Limits: the trade records are polymarket.com's, not Polymarket US's (whose price steps are 0.1c instead of 1c).
  The fills assume one trade covered our whole $20, which is generous, so the true losses are if anything larger.
  W1's bookmaker prices are snapshots, a median of about 6 hours of usable windows per market out of 24.

## What a pass would and would not mean

A pass needs TRAIN to choose a rule, VAL to confirm it, one look at TEST, and the skeptic to confirm the TEST result.
Only then is the rule **ready for pretend-money trading on the live desk**: a paper desk on Polymarket US at that
venue's own prices, until it has 100 round trips, after which the owner decides. It would not mean real money, that
the next months will repeat, that the US venue has enough buyers and sellers for $20 at those prices, or that the
venue lists the markets (W2's windows are not on Polymarket US; W4's may not be).

**Today: no desk reached VAL, no TEST was looked at, and no strategy is ready for pretend-money trading.**
