## result 0 

**Report: Pyth feed speed, plans, and what Kalshi actually settles on**
Research only. No accounts created, no credentials used, nothing written to the repo. All times Pacific (PDT). Live checks run 4:58–5:13 PM PT, Tue Oct 13, 2026 (a quiet hour; FX was open). Container egress was Columbus, Ohio, not Railway, so round-trip numbers are indicative only.

**Bottom line in plain words**

1. The "$2,500 plan" is real: Pyth Terminal's **Pro** tier, "Starting at $2,500/month (plus applicable tax)", "Up to 1ms update frequency", "Access to all symbols". It is the cheapest tier that includes FX. The **Starter** tier ($500/month) is "Up to 1 second" and "Access to all crypto symbols" only, so it does not help for EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD, USD/CAD. The **Free** tier has "No Pyth API access".
2. Even on Pro, not every FX pair is millisecond: Pyth's own reference data says EUR/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD support the `real_time` channel (updates "no faster than 1ms, no slower than 50ms"), but **GBP/USD and USD/CAD top out at `fixed_rate@200ms`** (one update every 200 ms).
3. There is no legitimate free or community copy of the fast Pyth feed any more. Pyth shut Pythnet/Pyth Core down (Sept 22, 2026), Hermes now returns "unauthorized" without a key (measured), and Triton's hosted Hermes is gone. Pyth Lazer was renamed Pyth Pro; it is the same keyed product.
4. The best keyless alternative found is **Kalshi itself**: a public, no-key REST endpoint returns the exact Pyth value Kalshi uses, as a 1-second series, about 0.2–0.5 s behind real time (measured). It is the settlement number (verified against a settled market), but it is 1-second data, not milliseconds. Kalshi also sells a `pyth_value` WebSocket channel ($1,000/month, first 7 days free) but its published list covers metals/commodities/indices, not FX.
5. Free market feeds measured (Kraken WebSocket, TrueFX) are either thin/off-price (Kraken: ~2 pips below Pyth, USD/JPY spread unusable) or slow (TrueFX keyless refreshes every 2.5–5.6 s). Neither is "as good as" Pyth Pro.

## result 1 

**FX feed speed check — plain-words report for the owner**
Measured Tue Oct 13, 2026, 5:09:58 PM to 5:24:58 PM Pacific (a quiet hour: 8:10–8:25 PM New York, Tokyo just opening). Research only: no accounts, no keys, no orders, nothing written to the repo. Raw data, scripts and `results.json` are in `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Claude/05bcdce0-3f75-5784-bd4b-6b2d4ca643ba/scratchpad/fx_feeds/` (see file list at the end).

**Bottom line**
1. There is no free, keyless feed that is "Pyth at millisecond speed." Pyth's own free stream (Hermes) now answers "401 unauthorized" — it has required a paid API key since Aug 26, 2026. Pyth's FX accounts on Solana mainnet exist but have been stale since Sep 28 (zero updates in our 15 minutes). The Pythnet RPCs refused us (403 / 502).
2. The $2,500/month plan is Pyth Pro's "FX (G10 only)" bundle. Pyth's own symbol list says that on Pro, EUR/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD are "real_time" (1–50 ms), but GBP/USD and USD/CAD are capped at one update per 200 ms. Also, Kalshi settles on the Core feed ("FX.EUR/USD"), which updates about every 400 ms — Pro is a faster sibling of that number, not the number itself.
3. Among free feeds, the one that actually tracks Pyth's price is TrueFX (bank-market quotes). At 5:15:00 PM PT, Kalshi's five new 15-minute markets opened with Pyth's prices as strikes; TrueFX was within 0.0–0.3 pips of Pyth on all five pairs, and Pyth's price sat inside TrueFX's bid/ask on four of five. The crypto exchanges' "EUR/USD" (Kraken, Bitstamp) were 1.5–2.6 pips away, Kraken USD/CAD 9 pips away, Kraken USD/JPY 94 pips away (its book was 3.5 yen wide). The stablecoin pairs carry a basis (USDT about +8.7 pips, USDC about −1.3 pips).
4. Kraken is by far the fastest and busiest free feed (62 ms to our box, up to 1,700 updates a minute) but it is a thin crypto-exchange book that trails bank quotes by roughly 1–2 seconds on EUR/USD and sits 2 pips off Pyth. TrueFX is only pollable (we polled every 0.5 s, the polite limit), so its quotes reach a bot about a quarter second late on average — yet by their own timestamps TrueFX showed both 1-pip EUR/USD moves first.

**Lead-lag on EUR/USD and GBP/USD (which feed shows a ≥1-pip move first)**
- Sample problem: EUR/USD and GBP/USD each moved only 1.5–1.9 pips in the whole 15 minutes, so there were just two 1-pip consensus moves on EUR/USD (5:10:30 PM up, 5:19:06 PM down) and one on GBP/USD that no feed printed cleanly. Medians below are over two events, so treat them as anecdotes.
- EUR/USD by the venues' own timestamps: TrueFX first both times; Bitstamp +37 ms and +1,745 ms; Kraken +1,389 ms and +1,990 ms; Coinbase USDC +1,332 ms (one event).
- EUR/USD by arrival at our box (what a bot sees): move 1 — TrueFX first, Coinbase USDC +1.20 s, Binance USDT +1.22 s, Kraken +1.30 s, Bitstamp +1.67 s; move 2 — Bitstamp first, TrueFX +20 ms, Kraken +1.93 s.
- Whole-window cross-correlation (more data, still weak signals): TrueFX leads Kraken by about 1.0–1.4 s on EUR/USD, 0.3–0.6 s on GBP/USD, 0.1–0.4 s on AUD/USD, ~2 s on USD/JPY (correlation only 0.06); USD/CAD shows Kraken ahead by ~1.8 s (correlation 0.07, not meaningful). TrueFX vs Bitstamp: no lead (0–0.1 s). Stablecoin pairs look 0.2–0.3 s ahead of TrueFX by arrival time — that is inside TrueFX's polling floor; by venue timestamps the lead disappears (−0.1 to 0.0 s).
- Half-pip moves (10 on GBP/USD): essentially coin flips between Kraken and TrueFX, with multi-second gaps either way, because TrueFX quotes arrive only every 2–13 s in this dead hour.
- Measurement floors: TrueFX polling adds 0–500 ms plus 70–300 ms request time to its arrival (its venue timestamps are exact to the millisecond). Our clock was 60 ms behind Kraken's server (43 ping samples, spread 48–72 ms; TrueFX Date headers bound it to −53…+134 ms), so absolute delays are good to about ±60 ms; lead-lag by arrival time needs no clock correction. Everything left this sandbox through an egress proxy near Ashburn (Kraken round trip 120 ms, so roughly 55–60 ms of every delay above is network path; Railway us-east4 should see similar or less, us-west2 more). The proxy also cut the Kraken tunnel twice (2 min 20 s and 11 min 49 s in); auto-reconnect took ~1.5 s each time, about 3–5 s of Kraken data lost per cut.

**Verdict (plain words)**
The fastest thing you can run tonight for free is still the keyless TrueFX page polled every half second: its bank quotes sat within a twentieth of a pip of Pyth all evening, and in this sample they showed where Kalshi's next published Pyth price was going about half a second to three-quarters of a second before Kalshi's own free 1-second series showed it (Kraken is quicker to deliver but sits 2 pips off and runs about a second behind; Kalshi's series is the exact settlement number but 0.2–0.5 s late). The only product that is literally "Pyth at millisecond speed" is Pyth Pro at "starting at $2,500/month" (confirm on the checkout screen that FX is included; even then GBP/USD and USD/CAD are capped at one update per 200 ms, and fees are non-refundable); the realistic cheaper approximations of the same bank-market number are OANDA's free practice stream (pushed, but conflated to 250 ms), Tiingo at $30 (pushed on every top-of-book change, unmeasured) and CME futures via Databento at about $199 plus a CME fee whose current amount I could not verify. On the half-second question: this sample contains no Kalshi order-book data at all, so it cannot say whether any feed beats the Kalshi books; it does say TrueFX leads Kalshi's public Pyth series by roughly half a second and that nothing here shows any free feed leading Pyth's real-time aggregate, which is what Pyth says Kalshi's market makers receive. The likeliest reading is that Kalshi's series is a slightly stale snapshot of Pyth, not that TrueFX sees the future, and the evidence is a dead-hour sample of 3–10 minutes with one move over half a pip, so repeat it in London/New York hours before spending anything.
